The project

The recycling programme gives customers a practical route for returning used genuine OKI consumables instead of treating them as ordinary waste. My portfolio records ongoing work supporting its promotion and visibility through digital content and campaign activity.

A key part of the communication was making eligibility clear. The service covers genuine OKI Original consumables such as toner cartridges, image drums (EP cartridges), fuser units and waste toner bottles.

My contribution

Turning sustainability into a usable service

Different return routes for different volumes

The practical detail matters. OKI’s public recycling guidance distinguishes smaller postal returns from larger collections, so the customer needs to understand not only why to recycle but which route to use.

Freepost

Smaller returns

Current OKI guidance describes Freepost for fewer than 10 genuine OKI Original consumable items, with a maximum package weight of 5 kg. Customers are instructed to package used consumables securely, particularly toner cartridges and image drums, to prevent leakage.

Recycling Bin Service

Larger or heavier returns

For more than 10 items, or items over the Freepost weight threshold, OKI directs customers to a Recycling Bin Service. Some heavier items, such as certain transfer belts, may need this route even when the number of items is small.

Return options and thresholds can vary by country. The details above reflect OKI’s current published UK/European guidance and should be checked against the appropriate local page before use.

The communication challenge

Sustainability messaging works best when it leads to a clear next step. For this project, the content had to move beyond a general environmental statement and answer the customer’s practical questions: which items qualify, whether the service is free, and how to return them.

For example, a customer with two toner cartridges has a different task from a business returning a large batch of used consumables. The content therefore needs to surface the decision points—item type, quantity, weight and local return method—without making the process feel complicated.

Another important distinction is authenticity. OKI’s current guidance states that non-original, compatible, refilled or other branded toners are not accepted through the OKI recycling service because their composition is not known to the programme. That makes “genuine OKI Original” more than a brand phrase; it is an operational eligibility rule that needs to be communicated clearly.

This is why the strongest sustainability communication on the page is actionable: it tells the customer what qualifies, how to package it, which route to use and where to continue.
