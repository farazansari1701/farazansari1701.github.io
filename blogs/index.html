Practical digital marketing lessons, with context

This is where I share the thinking behind my work in digital marketing: the questions a campaign needs to answer, the connections between channels, and the lessons that carry into the next project. I write from my professional experience at OKI Europe, while distinguishing personal reflections from general guidance that readers can adapt.

The aim is to make the decisions behind the work easier to understand. A tool name alone rarely explains a marketing challenge. The useful questions are who the activity serves, what the audience needs next, how delivery fits together, and what evidence supports the outcome.

Start with the customer journey

My first article explores B2B marketing automation and HubSpot lessons from OKI. It connects email, landing pages, CRM information and follow-up into one customer journey. You will find a direct explanation of automation, five practical lessons, a launch checklist and answers to common questions.

If you are planning an automated communication, use the checklist to review the audience, enrollment criteria, message, destination and next action. If you are reviewing an existing campaign, consider where those elements stop supporting one another. The article provides a starting point for those discussions rather than a ready-made configuration for every organisation.

How the case reflections are structured

I use Situation, Task, Action and Result to separate the context, the responsibility, the choices made and the outcome I can describe. That structure makes it easier to understand a lesson without confusing a professional learning outcome with an attributable commercial result.

Where a reflection covers an earlier period, the article identifies that period alongside its publication date. Quantified claims need supporting evidence; when an article does not establish a verified uplift, it says so. Practical suggestions are presented as guidance, with links to relevant product documentation where useful.
