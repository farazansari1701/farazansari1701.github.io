Cost per lead is useful because it puts a price against a campaign response. It becomes more useful when the next question is clear: what happened to those enquiries? A low acquisition cost can sit alongside poor contactability, an unclear requirement or a weak handover.

My work at OKI Europe spans paid campaigns, HubSpot and lead generation. Those responsibilities provide the context for this practical framework. It explains how to connect acquisition reporting with qualification, without presenting a platform lead as a customer or claiming an unverified revenue outcome.

## What is the best way to assess B2B lead quality?

Assess B2B lead quality by checking audience fit, the need expressed, contactability and progression through an agreed sales process. Review those signals alongside cost per lead. Define the stages, use a consistent reporting period and give enquiries enough time to progress before comparing campaigns.

Cost per lead equals the relevant campaign cost divided by the leads counted for that same scope and period. Cost per qualified lead uses the enquiries that meet an agreed qualification definition. Record both denominators clearly: a form submission, a new contact and a qualified lead can describe different things.

## Situation: acquisition and follow-up belong together

My [portfolio role history](/#work) describes work with digital campaigns at OKI Europe and, during my earlier role, lead enrichment and the sales funnel using HubSpot CRM. These responsibilities sit on different sides of the same practical problem: attracting a response and making the next interaction useful.

A campaign report can explain how efficiently people responded. A contact record can help explain who responded and what happened next. Bringing those views together requires compatible definitions. Otherwise, teams can debate performance while describing different groups of people.

## Task: define what a useful enquiry means

Before choosing a target, write down the action the campaign is intended to create. An educational download and a request for a product conversation express different levels of intent. Their lead counts should carry that context into the review.

Agree the minimum information needed for a useful next conversation. This could include the relevant market, organisation type, requirement and a suitable contact route. Keep the request proportionate to the offer. Collecting more fields does not automatically create stronger intent or better information.

## Action: review four connected signals

### Audience fit and expressed need

Compare the audience promised in the brief with the enquiries received. Ask whether the message attracted the intended organisations and whether the response describes a relevant need. Review patterns across a suitable sample instead of judging a campaign from one memorable conversation.

Separate a mismatch in targeting from a mismatch in the offer. Someone may fit the audience yet be researching a future requirement. That distinction changes whether the next step should be a sales conversation, useful information or a later review.

### Contactability and ownership

Check whether contact details are usable and whether responsibility for the next action is clear. A lead awaiting an owner tells a different story from an enquiry that was contacted and declined. Record these states separately so operational delays do not disappear inside a single conversion rate.

Before relying on those fields, use this [CRM data-quality review](/blogs/crm-data-quality-review-before-automation/) to check identity, consistency and the route information took into the system.

This connects with my earlier [HubSpot marketing automation lessons](/blogs/hubspot-marketing-automation-lessons-oki/): email, landing pages and CRM information need to support a coherent next step. Automation is most useful when its eligibility rules and ownership reflect the intended journey.

### Qualification and progression

[HubSpot’s lifecycle-stage documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/use-lifecycle-stages) distinguishes contacts qualified by marketing from those qualified by sales. Use those distinctions to agree the meaning of your own stages and the evidence required to move between them.

A practical review can then examine the share of eligible enquiries reaching each agreed stage. Keep the original acquisition group visible. Comparing this month’s newly acquired leads with opportunities from an older campaign would mix populations and obscure the time needed to progress.

### Cost and measurement boundaries

State which costs are included, which leads are counted and how duplicates are handled. Preserve the platform’s conversion definition alongside any CRM definition. Different attribution windows and counting rules can produce different totals without either system being broken.

[Google Ads’ offline-conversion guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10029210?hl=en-GB) describes qualified and converted leads as later steps measured outside the advertising platform. Where appropriate, that provides a route to connect campaign measurement with agreed downstream actions. Implementation needs accurate records and the necessary data permissions.

## Result: a clearer decision before a bigger claim

The useful output of this framework is a decision that identifies the next issue to investigate. Strong response volume with weak fit suggests reviewing the audience and offer. Relevant enquiries with little follow-up suggest reviewing ownership and process. Progression with rising acquisition costs suggests examining efficiency alongside value.

These are diagnostic possibilities, rather than proof that one change caused a commercial result. This article does not attribute a revenue uplift or qualification improvement to a specific OKI campaign. The reflection is that acquisition and follow-up become easier to discuss when their evidence is connected.

### Reflection: make the review useful to the next person

My recommendation is to finish a review with a clear question and a named next action. For example, if the team cannot distinguish enquiries waiting for contact from enquiries already assessed, improve that recording before drawing conclusions about targeting. The immediate benefit is a more interpretable report.

Preserve the context when sharing a finding with colleagues. Include the offer, market, stage definition and review period alongside the headline metric. This gives the next person enough information to challenge the interpretation, investigate an exception or repeat the comparison. A report earns its place in the process when it supports a decision that someone can follow through.

## A practical campaign-review checklist

- Define the offer, intended audience and action before comparing results.
- Record the lead definition, reporting period and cost scope.
- Check audience fit, contactability, ownership and stated need.
- Agree qualification criteria with the team receiving enquiries.
- Compare acquisition groups with equivalent time to progress.
- Document the decision, responsible person and next review date.

## Common questions about lead quality

### Is a lower cost per lead always better?

A lower cost per lead improves acquisition efficiency within the definition being measured. Assess fit and progression as well before deciding whether the campaign is creating the outcomes the brief requires.

### What should a small marketing team measure first?

Start with a reliable lead count, a documented qualification definition, clear ownership and a record of the next action. A modest report with consistent inputs is easier to act on than an elaborate dashboard with ambiguous stages.
