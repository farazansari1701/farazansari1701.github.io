A CRM field change is not a handover. The receiving team needs to know why the enquiry matters, who should act, what happens next and how the result will be recorded. Otherwise a dashboard can show progress while the conversation remains uncertain.

My approach is to treat the marketing-to-sales handover as a small agreement between people, supported by the CRM rather than replaced by it. This educational framework is not an employer case study or a claim of campaign results. It starts where my [lead-quality guide](/blog/b2b-lead-quality-cost-per-lead/) ends: after the enquiry has been assessed, what happens to it?

## The short answer: qualify, assign, accept, learn

A useful handover needs four separate decisions. **Qualification** says why this enquiry merits attention. **Assignment** identifies the next responsible person. **Acceptance** confirms that someone has taken responsibility for a timely action. **Feedback** records what happened, including why a record should return to nurture or be excluded from further outreach.

These are process definitions, not universal stages. HubSpot's [lifecycle-stage documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/use-lifecycle-stages) distinguishes a marketing-qualified lead from a sales-qualified lead: the latter has been qualified by sales. That difference is useful precisely because an automated stage change does not establish that a person has spoken to the prospect or checked the requirement.

## Agree what qualifies a handover

I would start with the decision the receiving colleague needs to make, rather than a scoring threshold borrowed from another organisation. A suitable handover record might include the expressed interest, the relevant product or service, market, source, date of the enquiry and any important limitations. These fields are meaningful only when their definitions are shared.

Ask three questions: does the person fit the audience; have they expressed a need; and is there enough reliable information to act? A good-fit contact may not be ready. A form completion cannot settle either point.

This connects with my [CRM data-quality review](/blog/crm-data-quality-review-before-automation/). A record that looks complete can still be duplicated, out of date or attached to the wrong company. Before assigning it, confirm the identity and check whether another owner or an open opportunity already exists. The point is to avoid sending a colleague into a conversation with misleading context.

## Give assignment and acceptance different meanings

An owner field answers, "Who is responsible?" It does not automatically answer, "Has this been picked up?" I would record the assignment time, a clear next-action expectation and a separate acceptance or acknowledgement. If a team uses an automated task, test whether the task reached the intended person and whether its due date is visible.

Define an exception route before launching the process. What happens if the assigned colleague is away, the account belongs to another territory, the record is already being worked or nobody accepts it? A queue without a named exception owner is a place where enquiries can disappear quietly. The rules should describe reassignment without sending duplicate notifications or inventing new leads.

The software is secondary. Salesforce's [lead qualification and routing lesson](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/opportunity-management/qualify-and-route-leads-to-your-reps) recommends involving sales in the definition of qualification and making the information needed for a decision available in the system. I take the broader lesson to be shared ownership of the decision, not simply an automated distribution rule.

## Keep lifecycle, outreach and opportunity distinct

A lifecycle stage, an outreach status and a deal stage answer different questions. HubSpot's [default contact-property reference](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-contact-properties) describes the contact fields; its [lead-record guidance](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/create-leads) explains that a lead object can represent a distinct sales process associated with a contact or company, depending on subscription and configuration.

For reporting, I would avoid silently treating those objects as interchangeable. A person may have one relationship history, several enquiries and more than one potential commercial conversation over time. Changing the lifecycle stage should not retroactively turn every enquiry into a new opportunity. Equally, a rejected handover should not erase the original response or the reason for rejection.

Where automation synchronises stages, check direction and scope. HubSpot documents [automatic lifecycle-stage settings](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/manage-how-lifecycle-stages-sync-between-objects), including possible changes from an associated company or deal. An apparently convenient toggle can alter records outside the intended handover if its rules are not reviewed first.

## Make the return path as clear as the forward path

Not every handed-over record will become a conversation, and not every conversation will become an opportunity. The useful question is what the receiving team has learned. I would keep a short, agreed set of outcomes: accepted and progressing, unable to contact, not the right fit, timing unsuitable, duplicate or more information needed. The labels must have definitions, not just names.

A returned record should carry a reason and an owner for the next decision. "Not now" might justify a permitted later review; "wrong contact" might call for correcting the record; an objection to marketing requires a different response altogether. My [workflow eligibility guide](/blog/hubspot-workflow-eligibility-suppression-rules/) explains why entry and exclusion rules should be tested independently rather than left implicit in a sequence.

## Check communication permissions before acting

A handover is not permission to send another email or message. The rules for processing personal data and the rules for sending electronic marketing must be considered together. The UK Information Commissioner's Office explains in its [B2B marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) that the position depends on the recipient and channel, and that objections and unsubscribe requests must be respected.

I would therefore keep the contact route, relevant permission or lawful-basis assessment, and suppression state visible to the person taking the next step. Do not infer consent from a populated phone number or email address. A commercial handover process should support responsible contact, not bypass the controls that protect it.

## Test with a fictional enquiry, then measure the handover

Imagine a fictional company requesting information through a website form. The CRM finds an existing company record but no open opportunity. The form states the product of interest, yet the intended market is unclear. Marketing can flag the missing field, route the record to the right review queue and avoid marking it sales accepted until the uncertainty is resolved.

Once corrected, the assigned colleague acknowledges the handover and records the next step. If contact cannot be made, that outcome is recorded without rewriting the original acquisition date. This fictional test illustrates why a handover needs onward and return routes.

For measurement, define separate denominators: eligible enquiries, assigned records, accepted handovers, attempts, meaningful conversations and opportunities. Record the date and scope for each count, and allow enough time for later outcomes to mature. My [attribution definitions guide](/blog/marketing-attribution-measurement-definitions/) explains why different time windows and counting units must not be blended into one persuasive-looking conversion rate.

## Questions worth asking before the process goes live

### What makes a lead sales accepted?

An explicit, documented acceptance decision by the receiving team. A routing notification or lifecycle update can be evidence of assignment, but neither proves the next person accepted or worked the record.

### What if sales returns an enquiry?

Keep the original record, capture a specific reason, and choose a responsible next action. Do not automatically re-enrol the person in marketing messages without checking the relevant preferences and rules.

### What should the first report show?

Start with eligible, assigned, accepted and returned handovers, each with a clear definition and period. Add downstream outcomes only when the underlying records can support them. A smaller accurate report is more useful than one that exaggerates certainty.

## A handover is an agreement, not a field change

The practical aim is simple: when responsibility moves, clarity should move with it. Define qualification together, make acceptance visible, give exceptions an owner and close the feedback loop. That creates a process people can explain and improve without claiming that a task completion, email click or CRM label is a sale.
