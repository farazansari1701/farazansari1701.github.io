A notebook for looking a little closer

A poem, an old street, a piece of music or a conversation can leave me thinking long after the moment has passed. This is the space I am making for those thoughts: how we come to see the world as we do, and what might change if we looked again.

Poetry, art and music

I am interested in what a few words can hold, and in the feelings that resist a neat explanation. Poetry makes room for ambiguity. Art and music offer other ways of paying attention: to a detail, a silence, a feeling I cannot yet name. I want to explore why certain things stay with us, and how something made by another person can feel unexpectedly close to our own lives.

These interests also shape how I want to write. An image or a line of verse can be a starting point for reflection without becoming a lesson to take away. Sometimes it is enough to notice what moved us and spend a little longer with the question.

History, travel and belonging

My journey connects Varanasi and Mussoorie with Cambridge and London. That gives this notebook a starting point, but there is more to a place than my experience of it. I am curious about the histories beneath familiar streets, the stories a city chooses to remember, and the people whose lives are missing from the version a visitor first encounters.

Travel brings those questions into focus. What makes somewhere feel like home? What do we carry from one place to another? I want to think about belonging as something shaped by language, memory, relationships and the ordinary details of daily life, as well as by a location on a map.

Social issues and the lives behind an argument

Fairness, dignity and freedom keep bringing me back to people. A public debate can become very certain very quickly, while the lives it concerns remain complicated. I want to ask whose perspective is being heard, whose choices are constrained, and what I might be overlooking from where I stand.

This is also a reason to question my own assumptions. Empathy does not mean every disagreement disappears. It means taking another person's experience seriously enough to listen, consider the context and resist reducing them to a position I agree or disagree with.

How to enter this notebook

Start with More Questions Than Answers, my first essay about uncertainty, identity and the freedom to question. To understand the experiences behind this notebook, read about my move to the UK in 2019 or get to know me. Each piece starts with something I have genuinely reflected on, leaving room for uncertainty rather than forcing a tidy conclusion.

I will distinguish what I observed from what I infer, give factual claims their context and respect other people's privacy. To explore the professional side of the same curiosity, visit all my writing. You can also follow new writing through the RSS feed.
