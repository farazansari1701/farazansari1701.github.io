What HubSpot taught me about marketing automation

Lessons from my early OKI work on connecting email, landing pages and CRM — and making the next step clearer.

Notes on the decisions, tools and lessons behind the work.

When I joined OKI Europe in October 2022, my work brought together email, website content, lead enrichment and HubSpot marketing automation. Each activity had its own requirements, but they all touched the same customer journey.

An email could create interest. A landing page could help someone understand the offer. A CRM record could provide context for the next conversation. The quality of the experience depended on how those pieces connected.

Looking back at my Digital Marketing Executive role, from October 2022 to September 2025, that is the lesson I return to most: good automation starts with understanding the next step a person needs to take.

The short answer

HubSpot marketing automation is most useful when it connects relevant communication, clear website journeys and useful CRM information. My experience at OKI taught me to think about the purpose and handover behind each activity, alongside the platform features used to deliver it.

Situation: several channels, one customer journey

My responsibilities at OKI included marketing and communications automations, email activity, landing-page optimisation and lead enrichment through HubSpot CRM. I also worked on website implementation, regular updates and troubleshooting, alongside multichannel marketing for B2B and B2C audiences across EMEA.

That breadth made the relationship between channels especially relevant. A campaign message does not exist independently of the page it links to. Information captured through a digital experience becomes part of the context available for subsequent engagement.

It is easy to focus on the asset immediately in front of you: the email that needs building or the page that needs updating. My work across these areas gave me a reason to consider what happened on either side of that asset. That wider perspective became an important part of my development.

Task: connect delivery with a useful next step

My role was to support marketing delivery through automation, content and CRM work. This meant creating communications and helping maintain the digital experiences and contact information around them.

The practical challenge was to connect execution with purpose. Who was the communication intended for? What would make its next step relevant? What information would help someone continue the conversation?

Those questions remain useful whether the activity is an automated follow-up, a landing page or a CRM update. They make it possible to assess a task as part of a journey, rather than simply confirming that it has been completed.

Action: what the work taught me to look for

1. Give automation a clear job

I used HubSpot to create marketing and communications automations. That experience helped me connect the platform's capabilities with the communication requirement behind the work.

An automation needs a clear purpose. “Send another email” describes an action; “help someone understand the next step” describes an intention. The distinction matters because it changes how you assess whether the communication is useful.

HubSpot’s documentation explains how workflows use enrolment criteria and actions to automate processes. My takeaway is to understand the intended journey before concentrating on the mechanics of building it.

2. Treat the landing page as part of the message

My responsibilities included optimising landing-page content, structure and design. This reinforced the connection between attracting a response and helping someone act on it.

A useful review question is whether the page continues the promise made by the email or campaign. If someone arrives expecting information about a particular offer, the relevance should be easy to recognise.

For me, this is where content and conversion thinking meet. A clear page supports understanding as well as action. The layout, wording and next step all contribute to that experience.

3. Use testing to ask a focused question

My work also included A/B testing within outbound marketing. Testing encourages a valuable habit: treating the first version as something that can be improved.

The principle I take from this is to be clear about what a test is intended to reveal. A different version should help answer a question about the communication, rather than simply create another variation.

It is also worth distinguishing the immediate response from the wider objective. A click can be useful evidence about engagement, while the subsequent journey needs its own assessment.

4. Think about the person using the CRM record

Lead enrichment and sales-funnel management through HubSpot CRM formed another part of my responsibilities. This brought the information behind a contact into the same picture as the marketing activity.

The question I find useful is simple: what would help the next person understand this enquiry? It encourages a view of CRM information as something people need to use, rather than a collection of fields to complete.

That perspective also helps connect marketing with subsequent engagement. The handover deserves attention because a customer journey can continue across teams, tools and conversations.

5. Keep the experience working after launch

Alongside campaign activity, I worked on website updates, maintenance and troubleshooting. My experience also included converting UX designs into a functioning website with integrated CMS and CRM capabilities.

This added an operational dimension to my marketing work. Delivering a page is one stage in its life. Keeping its content relevant and its key functions working is part of supporting the experience over time.

It is a lesson I now carry into wider campaign thinking: delivery and maintenance belong in the same conversation.

Result: a foundation for broader campaign ownership

The result I can describe from this period is a practical foundation across automation, email, websites and CRM. My contribution included creating communications, improving landing pages, maintaining digital experiences and supporting lead enrichment.

This is a reflection on that body of work, rather than a claim that one workflow produced a particular revenue or conversion uplift. The value of the experience was in learning how the different responsibilities related to one another.

In October 2025, I progressed to Senior Digital Marketing Executive at OKI Europe. My earlier role gave me relevant experience for broader responsibilities, although that career milestone reflects a wider contribution than automation alone.

Reflection: learn the tool and the journey

The lesson I would share with another marketer is to develop platform knowledge alongside an understanding of the customer experience.

Before building, identify the purpose. While delivering, consider the connection between the message, page and contact information. Afterwards, review what worked and what needs attention.

For B2B marketing automation, this creates a useful starting point: make the next step clear, relevant and supported by enough context for the conversation to continue.

That is the perspective my HubSpot work at OKI helped me develop. It continues to shape how I think about digital marketing: as connected experiences that need careful execution, useful information and ongoing improvement.
